Why "there's nothing to be scared of" doesn't work

Between ages 3 and 7, a child's imagination grows faster than their ability to tell what's real. A shadow becomes a monster. A creak becomes a stranger. Their fear is real to them, and when we say "it's nothing," they only hear that they're alone with it.

What scared children actually need at bedtime are three things:

1To know they're not aloneEvery night, Ellie and Sam face the same fear your child has. Kids learn best from characters like them. When Sam is brave, your child believes they can be too.
2Something to do when they're scaredA simple prayer and a brave breath they can use on their own, even at 2am when you're asleep down the hall.
3Someone bigger watching over themA short Bible verse each night, so the last thought before sleep isn't "what's in the dark?" but "God is with me."

That's exactly what each 5-minute night gives them, one fear at a time, for 30 nights, until bravery becomes a habit.

A faith they can hold in the dark

This isn't only about getting through bedtime. Every night, your child hides one more verse in their heart and learns to talk to God in their own words. Scared at 4, nervous at 9, anxious at 15: they'll already know where to turn.
